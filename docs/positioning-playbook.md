# Positioning playbook

How to frame the same career for different roles without changing a single fact. Positioning is chosen from signals in the posting, then applied to the summary, bullet order and answers.

## 1. Read the posting's signals first

| Signal in the posting | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| "Hands-on", "individual contributor", "you will build", no direct reports | IC role: lead with what you build, not who you manage |
| "Lead a team of", "build and grow the function" | Manager role: lead with scope, team and decisions |
| Years asked (e.g. "5-8 years") well below your own | Drop the years, or you read as overqualified and expensive |
| "No job titles", "flat", "ex-founders", "high velocity" | Startup culture: humble, ambitious, fast; no seniority signals |
| Function the team sits in (Finance, Operations, Product) | Name the matching part of your experience (e.g. "team within Finance") |
| What the posting lists first | The screener's first check; your first 2-3 bullets must answer it |

## 2. Summary headline patterns

The first noun phrase is an **honest label** of who you are. It never copies the posting's job title (that reads forced and invites probing). Mirror the posting in sentence 2 and in the bullets instead.

| Role type | Sentence 1 (headline) | Sentence 3 ("Known for...") |
|---|---|---|
| Manager / leadership | "Commercial analytics leader and MBA with 17+ years at Company A and Company B." | "...building strong teams and turning analysis into recommendations that leaders act on." |
| IC, senior | "Hands-on advanced analytics leader and MBA with 17+ years at..."; sentence 2 starts with "Build..." | "...turning complex data into executive-ready recommendations." The team moves into the first bullet. |
| IC, fewer years asked | "Hands-on commercial analytics professional and MBA, with experience at Company A and Company B." | Same as IC |
| Startup / flat org | "Hands-on analytics builder and MBA with a deep record of shipping models and tools at Company A and Company B." No years, no "leader". | "...delivering results fast and driving innovation beyond assigned scope." |
| Finance-function role | Sentence 2 names the team's place: "Lead the analytics team within Finance..." | "...building models Finance signs off on." |
| Strategy / consulting | Lead with structured problem solving and the consulting years | "...structuring ambiguous problems." |

Rule of three: at most three sentences, no word repeated across sentences, no user counts or one-off executive name-drops.

## 3. Bridge, don't disclaim

When your experience is adjacent, describe it in the posting's vocabulary instead of apologising for it.

- A digital marketplace's inventory has **availability** and **quality**, just like a physical supply chain. Say "restored availability" and "raised supply quality", not "although my supply experience is digital rather than physical".
- A pension valuation is a lifetime-value calculation: survival factors turn an annuity into a lump sum. That bridges to customer lifetime value and cohort economics.
- Commercial target-setting with tiered rebates is forecasting under uncertainty with actuals to compare against.

The bridge changes the words, never the facts. A tool or method you haven't used stays a gap in the fit analysis; it never appears on the CV.

## 4. Bullet order

1. The team/scope bullet (one or two lines).
2. Two or three bullets with **outcomes** that answer the posting's first requirements.
3. Bullets that match keywords but carry no outcome go lower, or get merged with one that does.
4. Keep the CV at its length: every added bullet replaces the weakest one.

## 5. Culture-specific choices

- **Startups:** drop years and seniority words; lead with speed and shipping; show the team "worked like a startup inside a large company" only if it is true.
- **Humble cultures:** prefer "builder" to "leader"; show traits through stories ("what I enjoy most is..."), never blunt self-labels ("I have a bias to action").
- **"Why us" questions:** personality wins over mission restatement. A light, true joke is better than praise.
