# Writing rules

House style for every CV, cover letter and form answer. Each rule came from a real correction.

## Facts
- Every claim (number, tool, method, outcome, team size) traces to the master CV, the content library, the story bank or my own words. If a detail would help but isn't documented, ask.
- Numbers are written exactly as approved, with their scope ("in the first year", "for traffic in scope"). Two separate wins are never merged into one cause and effect.
- "My team" for team work, "I" for my own work.
- Efficiency figures never stand alone: pair them with the outcome ("replacing days of manual work while improving time-to-insight").
- Never claim the result of other people's work (e.g. sales attainment); claim the decisions leaders made, the adoption and the efficiency.

## CV bullets
- Present-tense verb first for current work; past tense for past roles.
- One line of scope, then a concrete result. No colon lists, no buzzword stacks.
- Maximum three rendered lines; never a single orphan word on the last line (checked by `scripts/check_cv_layout.py`).
- Capitalize departments when you work with them as a team (Finance, Product, Engineering); lower case when descriptive ("product gaps").
- Spell out internal abbreviations and company nicknames.

## Cover letters
- About 300-350 words, one page. Crisp means fewer words, not fewer facts.
- Hook: two short, factual sentences on what I do and enjoy, specific to the posting. No slogans; "cheesy" openings get cut.
- Three or four proof points, each with a bold headline that carries a number.
- Use "demonstrate", not "show". Lead into the closing with a connecting clause so it isn't abrupt.
- Skip the letter entirely when the application doesn't ask for one.

## Form answers
- Answer in the order the question asks (e.g. assumptions, then actuals).
- Name the hard part explicitly ("The hardest edge case was...") so a screener can't miss it.
- State the conclusion in one line ("a timing effect, not lost demand").
- One paragraph per idea: context, method, result, what changed afterwards.
- A real story close to the question beats a polished story that is off-topic.
- Technical questions stay plain; "why us" questions can carry personality.

## Tone
- Measured, concrete, humble. Show traits through the story, framed as what I enjoy.
- Casual words ("way beyond") are a deliberate choice for informal cultures; flag them for formal ones.
